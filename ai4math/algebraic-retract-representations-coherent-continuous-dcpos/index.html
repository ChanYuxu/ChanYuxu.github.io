Algebraic retract representations of coherent continuous dcpos

continuous dcpos · algebraic retracts · coherence · Lawson compactness

All content in this section is AI-generated. AI may make mistakes; please verify important information carefully.

Every continuous dcpo is a Scott-continuous retract of an algebraic dcpo. What can be preserved when choosing the algebraic dcpo? For a coherent continuous dcpo, the representing dcpo can be chosen coherent, with a lower-finite poset of compact elements. If the original dcpo is Lawson compact or has a countable basis, the representation can retain those properties too.

Theorem 1.1. Every coherent continuous dcpo D admits Scott-continuous maps s: D → A and r: A → D with r ∘ s = idD, where A is coherent and algebraic and its compact-element poset is lower finite. If D is Lawson compact, A can also be chosen Lawson compact.

Here “lower finite” means that each compact element of A has only finitely many compact elements below it. No least element is assumed.

Why the usual ideal completion is not enough

Coherence says that the intersection of two Scott-compact saturated sets is again Scott compact. Even if an arbitrary basis lies in a coherent dcpo, the common upper bounds of two basis elements within the basis need not be generated upwards by finitely many basis elements. The construction therefore takes the ideal completion of a different poset: finite systems of approximants, ordered by restriction.

Finite systems and exact refinements

Coherence provides finitely many choices of common approximants for the configurations used in a finite system. A system p has a Scott-open set Up on which its data are valid. For two systems p and q, any point where both are valid also admits a common refinement from a fixed finite family. In particular,

Up ∩ Uq = ⋃ { Ur | r is a chosen common refinement of p and q }.

The nontrivial inclusion combines the two systems at a point in the intersection. For each point of D, the systems valid there form an ideal. Sending a point to this ideal gives the section; taking the directed supremum of the approximants recorded by an ideal gives the retraction.

Lawson compactness and countable bases

If D is Lawson compact, Scott compactness gives a finite cover by validity opens. Restricting the system poset to the upward closure of the selected systems preserves the retraction and gives property M: for each finite subset, the set of minimal upper bounds is finite, and every upper bound is above one of them. The ideal completion of the restricted poset is then Lawson compact.

Starting with a countable basis leaves only countably many finite systems. Their principal ideals give a countable compact basis for the representing dcpo; the Lawson-compact restriction also preserves countability.

Applications

Function spaces. If D is Lawson compact and continuous and R is an RB-domain, then [R → D] is Lawson compact and continuous, while [D → R] is an RB-domain. The proof applies the algebraic function-space theorems to spaces of maps between the representing dcpos, then transports the results along the induced retractions.

Prefinite approximation. A dcpo is Lawson compact and continuous exactly when its identity is the directed supremum of Scott-continuous maps below it, each factoring through a prefinite algebraic dcpo. The stage dcpos need not be finite: their compact elements are generated from a finite set by iterating minimal upper bounds, and their compact-element posets have property M. In this characterization, an increasing sequence of maps suffices exactly when the dcpo has a countable basis.
