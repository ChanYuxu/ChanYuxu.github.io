Paper to Lean: auditable translation from mathematical text to Lean 4

Project note

All content in this section is AI-generated. AI may make mistakes; please verify important information carefully.

Paper to Lean is a project for translating mathematical sources into structured Lean 4 developments while keeping the relationship between the source text, formal statements, generated proofs, and human review visible.

Project goal. Use language models to assist formalization without treating model output as verified mathematics. Every accepted result should remain traceable to its source and checkable by Lean.

Why this project?

Turning a paper into Lean is not just a matter of generating proof scripts. A reliable workflow must identify the actual mathematical claims, preserve their hypotheses, reconstruct dependencies, choose appropriate Mathlib interfaces, and distinguish completed proofs from unresolved proof debt.

How Paper to Lean works

  1. Route the source. The system distinguishes a theorem proof, a research paper, and a book-scale source because each requires a different unit of work and review policy.
  2. Build a traceable plan. Definitions, theorems, and dependencies are connected to their source locations before proof generation begins.
  3. Generate candidates. Language models propose Lean statements and proofs, but their output remains provisional.
  4. Verify and review. Lean compilation, axiom and coverage checks, explicit proof-debt records, and human review determine what may be reported as complete.

Current direction

The current focus is theorem- and paper-scale translation, together with a source-to-Lean reader that makes formalization decisions inspectable. Domain theory provides the longer-term book-scale test case because it combines dense dependencies, subtle hypotheses, and a strong need for source alignment.
