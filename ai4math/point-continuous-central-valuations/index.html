Point-Continuous and Central Valuations

Countable spaces · Sorgenfrey-Smyth dcpo · Fubini objects

All content in this section is AI-generated. AI may make mistakes; please verify important information carefully.

Continuous valuations are a domain-theoretic model of probabilistic choice. Their Fubini problem asks when two valuations can be integrated in either order, even though the Scott topology on a product dcpo may be finer than the ordinary product topology. The manuscript summarized here gives positive results for countable spaces, the Smyth dcpo of the Sorgenfrey line, and dcpos with second-countable Scott topology.

Main results. If every open set of a space is the saturation of an at most countable subset, then every continuous valuation is point-continuous. The Smyth dcpo D = Q(ℝℓ) of the Sorgenfrey line is a Fubini object, although it carries a continuous valuation that is not point-continuous. Moreover, if the Scott-open set lattice σ(P) is a Fubini object, then P is one; in particular, every dcpo with second-countable Scott topology is a Fubini object.

Point-continuity, centrality, and the Fubini problem

A continuous valuation assigns a nonnegative mass to each open set, satisfies modularity, and preserves directed unions. Point-continuity strengthens this by requiring the mass of an open set to be detectable from finitely many points: whenever r < μ(U), there is a finite set F ⊆ U such that μ(V) > r for every open neighbourhood V of F.

For a dcpo P, a valuation is central when it commutes with every continuous valuation on every partner dcpo under iterated Choquet integration. A dcpo is called a Fubini object when all its continuous valuations are central. Point-continuous valuations are known to be central, but the converse fails in the Sorgenfrey-Smyth example below.

Why countable spaces force finite witnesses

For a subset A of a space and a valuation ν, the manuscript considers the neighbourhood capacity

κν(A) = inf { ν(V) : A ⊆ V and V is open }.

If an at most countable set A is exhausted by increasing finite sets F1 ⊆ F2 ⊆ ···, modularity and directed continuity give κν(A) = supn κν(Fn). When an open set U is the saturation of A, the sets A and U have the same open neighbourhoods, so κν(A) = ν(U). One finite stage therefore supplies the witness required for point-continuity.

This proves that every continuous valuation on a countable space is point-continuous. For dcpos, the argument applies whenever every Scott-open set has an at most countable order-coinitial subset. Consequently every countable dcpo satisfies V(P) = P(P) = Z(P).

The Sorgenfrey-Smyth dcpo is not a counterexample to Fubini

Let ℝℓ be the Sorgenfrey line and let D = Q(ℝℓ) be its nonempty compact subsets, ordered by reverse inclusion. Its Scott topology is the upper Vietoris topology. Goubault-Larrecq and Jia constructed a continuous valuation on D that is not point-continuous and suggested that this space might help produce a counterexample to general Fubini equations.

The manuscript proves the opposite conclusion for this particular dcpo: every continuous valuation on D is central. Thus the three classes are separated precisely as

P(D) ⊊ Z(D) = V(D).

The proof first lifts each finite valuation on D to a finite τ-smooth Borel measure on the full Vietoris space. A countable family of minimum and maximum observations makes that space first-countable and produces a countable family of inner approximating opens. A Scott-continuous Fatou inequality, finite measurable partitions, and a Radon-Nikodym argument then establish the required incidence identity. Bounded approximation extends the result from finite valuations to arbitrary continuous valuations.

Canonical incidence and descent

The centrality test is reduced to the canonical incidence relation between points and Scott-open sets. If Mx = {U ∈ σ(P) : x ∈ U}, then a valuation μ on P is central exactly when every valuation ξ on σ(P) satisfies

∫σ(P) μ(U) dξ(U) = ∫P ξ(Mx) dμ(x).

This formulation also gives a descent theorem: if σ(P) is a Fubini object, then so is P. Combining the descent with the co-consonance theorem for open-set lattices of countably based spaces yields the second-countable consequence. The manuscript explicitly notes that the result is not confined to the already known core-compact case.

The generalized appendix

The appendix works in an arbitrary subspace Y of the countable product ℝℓℕ, with its ordinary product-subspace topology. If f : Y → ΣP is continuous and ν is any continuous valuation on Y, then the pushforward f*ν is a central valuation on P. Neither valuation is required to have finite mass, and no closedness or ambient Borel measurability assumption is imposed on Y.

This statement concerns the centrality of a pushforward on the target dcpo. It does not identify the ordinary product topology on Y with a Scott topology, nor does it call Y itself a Fubini object. The proof uses discrete threshold minorants, a countable laminar family with a one-sided density property, a measure-extension argument for spaces with a clopen basis, and bounded approximation. Applied to a product representation of the full Vietoris space, it gives a second proof that the Sorgenfrey-Smyth dcpo is a Fubini object.
