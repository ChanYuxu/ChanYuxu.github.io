Finite separation on subprobability powerdomains

FS-domains · continuous valuations · finite separation

All content in this section is AI-generated. AI may make mistakes; please verify important information carefully.

Continuous valuations provide a domain-theoretic model of probabilistic choice. This manuscript proves that the subprobability powerdomain preserves FS-domains, using finite separators on the source to construct finitely separated approximations on the valuation space. The accompanying Lean 4 development records the full construction and its principal examples.

Main result. The subprobability powerdomain construction preserves FS-domains: whenever D is an FS-domain, V≤1(D) is also an FS-domain.

Why finite separation matters

An FS-domain carries a directed approximate identity of Scott-continuous maps that are finitely separated from the identity. For a map f : D → D, finite separation means that there is a finite set M such that every x admits some a ∈ M with f(x) ≤ a ≤ x. The image of f need not itself be finite.

The probabilistic question is whether this finite control can be lifted from points of D to continuous subprobability valuations. This addresses a closure problem in probabilistic domain theory: the powerdomain construction should preserve the approximation structure needed for higher-order probabilistic semantics.

The finite-order construction

The proof begins on the basis of simple valuations. A finite separator M determines a finite poset of source slices, ordered by reverse inclusion. Height-dependent thresholds select the parts of an input valuation that are sufficiently visible at each level. A correction term along cover relations restores monotonicity when the selected subset changes.

The resulting local map keeps the atoms at the points f2(x) rather than replacing the valuation by an element of a fixed finite-poset powerdomain. The finite poset controls the comparison, but it is not used as an unproved factorization object. A fixed scale leaves a positive margin, and the separating witness is moved to M and rounded down on one finite rational grid:

local monotone map → fixed positive margin → finite grid witness → uniform finite separator.

This distinction is important: moving atoms to M constructs the finite separating witness; it is not part of the approximating map itself and does not require a monotone choice of representatives.

From simple valuations to all valuations

Simple subprobability valuations form a basis of V≤1(D) when D is continuous. Each monotone basis map therefore extends by taking the directed supremum of its values on simple valuations way-below the input. The same finite set continues to separate the extension from the identity.

Finite simultaneous approximation then supplies one source approximation and one threshold level that work for any finite collection of way-below requirements. Composition squares turn these extensions into a directed approximate identity. This avoids assuming monotonicity in the numerical threshold or compatibility between independently chosen finite separators.

Examples and edge cases

The manuscript tests the construction on chains and a four-element diamond. The examples expose why exit corrections, unions of overlapping upper sets, strict thresholds, and a fixed scale are needed.

The singleton example also explains why the selection test must be strict. Replacing “greater than” by “greater than or equal to” creates a jump at the threshold and destroys Scott continuity of the local formula.
