Unit-interval retracts in Lean 4

Formalization case study

This example follows one counterexample from its mathematical manuscript to a checked Lean 4 development. It is also a small test of what an auditable paper-to-Lean workflow should make visible: the source result, the formal declarations, the dependencies, and the remaining boundary between them.

Main result. The construction gives a quasicontinuous complete lattice P that is neither continuous nor quasialgebraic. Every chain in P is at most countable, and there is a Scott-continuous surjection P → [0,1], but [0,1] neither order-embeds into P nor occurs as a Scott-continuous retract of it. In particular, it gives negative answers to two public problems posed by Xiaoquan Xu.

Xu's public questions

In Open Problems in Domain Theory, Xiaoquan Xu posed Problems 1.14 and 1.15 (p. 33) for a quasicontinuous domain P that is not quasialgebraic:

  1. Must [0,1] be a Scott-continuous retract of P?
  2. Must P contain a sub-dcpo order-isomorphic to [0,1]?

The manuscript answers both questions in the negative through one concrete counterexample. It also separates these two stronger properties from the existence of a Scott-continuous surjection onto [0,1]. Its key technical ingredient is an exact description of finite-set approximation in a sparse interval construction.

What is formalized

The downloadable source contains the public aggregate file and sixteen component modules. They develop the interval geometry, complete-lattice structure, finite-set approximation, quasicontinuity, noncontinuity, counterexample, and dyadic splitting arguments. The declaration UnitIntervalRetracts.mainCounterexample collects the properties of the concrete lattice, while UnitIntervalRetracts.negativeAnswers records the two negative conclusions.

A separate finite-grid module formalizes the compactness step behind the positive splitting criterion: compatible choices on all finite dyadic grids yield a Scott-continuous section.

Verification status

The focused project build succeeds with Lean v4.30.0-rc2 and Mathlib v4.30.0-rc2. Across the 3,809 lines in this bundle, the current audit finds no sorry, admit, local axiom, opaque, or unsafe. An axiom audit of the main counterexample, its negative corollary, and the finite-grid splitting theorem reports only the standard Lean/Mathlib dependencies propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound.

This status applies to the Lean declarations in the bundle. It does not mean that the manuscript's prose, bibliography, or typesetting has itself been formalized. The source archive is also an extract that depends on the surrounding domain-theory project.
