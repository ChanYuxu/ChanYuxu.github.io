FS-domains are not always RB-domains

Yuxu Chen, Hui Kou, and Zhenchao Lyu

For more than three decades, it was unknown whether FS-domains and RB-domains describe the same class of domains. Our recent work gives a negative answer by proving that a classical geometric domain is FS but not RB.

Main result. Lawson's planar closed-disk domain is an FS-domain, but it is not an RB-domain. Consequently, the class of RB-domains is strictly contained in the class of FS-domains.

The question

RB-domains and FS-domains are two important classes used to describe approximation in domain theory. Every RB-domain is known to be an FS-domain, but the converse had remained open since FS-domains were introduced in 1990. The problem was therefore to find either a general construction turning every FS-domain into an RB-domain, or one explicit counterexample.

The counterexample

The counterexample is Lawson's domain of closed disks in the Euclidean plane. Its elements are the closed disks together with the whole plane as a bottom element, ordered by reverse inclusion. This domain was already known to be an FS-domain and had long been regarded as a natural candidate for separating the two notions.

We prove that the disk domain cannot carry the finite approximation structure required of an RB-domain. The argument translates the geometry of disks into the Lorentz cone in dimension 2+1 and studies an associated matrix cone. That geometric obstruction rules out the required RB approximations.

Why the result matters

The result settles a long-standing structural question in domain theory and shows that the flexibility of FS approximation is genuinely stronger than RB approximation. It also provides a concrete example where order-theoretic approximation is decided through convex and Lorentzian geometry.
