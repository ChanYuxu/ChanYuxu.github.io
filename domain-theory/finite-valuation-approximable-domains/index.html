Finite-valuation approximable domains and the Jung–Tix problem

Yuxu Chen, Hui Kou, and Zhenchao Lyu

Higher-order probabilistic semantics needs two structures at once: function spaces for higher-order computation and valuation powerdomains for probabilistic choice. For nearly three decades, the Jung–Tix problem has asked whether these requirements can coexist inside a natural full category of domains. Our new work gives a positive answer to its category-existence, or generalized, form.

Main result. We introduce the category ωFVA of finite-valuation approximable domains. It is a full Cartesian closed subcategory of pointed countably based FS-domains and is closed under both the subprobability and probability valuation powerdomains. Consequently, both valuation monads restrict to ωFVA.

The compatibility problem

Cartesian closed categories provide function-space objects [X → Y] and therefore support higher-order types. Continuous valuations, on the other hand, provide the probabilistic powerdomains V≤1(D) and V1(D). The difficulty is that the class of all continuous domains is not Cartesian closed, while the familiar Cartesian closed subclasses with strong finite-approximation properties were not known to be preserved by probabilistic powerdomains.

Jung and Tix isolated this tension in the 1990s. Their finite-tree results showed both the promise of finite approximation and the difficulty of extending it. The present paper addresses the category-existence formulation: it constructs a nontrivial full Cartesian closed class of continuous domains on which the two valuation constructions remain internal. This is the precise sense in which the paper gives a positive solution; it does not assert that every FS-domain or every RB-domain is preserved by probabilistic powerdomains.

First step: probabilistic powerdomains of finite posets

The first part of the proof settles a basic finite case. For every finite poset P, the subprobability valuation domain V≤1(P) is a countably based FS-domain. The proof constructs an explicit increasing FS approximate identity by moving valuation mass downward from the maximal points of its current support. Carefully separated erosion rates keep the construction monotone for the stochastic order.

The probability case has a sharp boundary: for a finite nonempty poset Q, V1(Q) is an FS-domain exactly when Q has a least element. Without a least element, the probability powerdomain contains infinitely many minimal valuations, which no finite separator can cover.

The new approximation principle

The finite valuation spaces are then used as building blocks for general domains. A domain D is finite-valuation approximable when its identity is the pointwise supremum of an increasing sequence of maps of the form

Approximation pattern. D → V≤1(Pn) → D, where each Pn is finite and the resulting self-maps increase pointwise to the identity.

The first map can be read as a finite probabilistic encoding and the second as a reconstruction. Unlike the finite-image deflations used to define RB-domains, the intermediate valuation space is generally infinite, and no retraction equation is required. What remains finite is the underlying order data controlling each approximation stage.

The closure theorem

A finite-separation saturation theorem and a unified stochastic-kernel lifting argument establish the required closure properties:

  • every object of ωFVA is a pointed countably based FS-domain;
  • the category contains the terminal dcpo and is closed under Scott-continuous retracts and finite products;
  • if X and Y belong to ωFVA, then the function space [X → Y] also belongs to ωFVA;
  • both V≤1(D) and V1(D) remain in ωFVA.

Thus higher-order functions and probabilistic effects coexist in one full category with all Scott-continuous maps as morphisms.

Where the new class sits

The new class is strictly larger than the countably based bc-domains and strictly smaller than the pointed countably based FS-domains. It is also incomparable with the class of RB-domains: some finite-valuation approximable domains are not RB-domains, while a finite RB-domain constructed in the paper is not finite-valuation approximable. This shows that the construction is not merely a reformulation of an existing approximation class.

Why the result matters

The main conceptual move is to replace finite-image approximation by approximation through valuation spaces generated by finite posets. This retains finite combinatorial control while allowing the intermediate objects to carry genuine probabilistic structure. The resulting category provides a new domain-theoretic setting in which higher-order function types and probabilistic choice are simultaneously available.
